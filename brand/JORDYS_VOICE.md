# JORDYS_VOICE.md

The master system doc says what Jordy's *is*. This file says how it's *written* — the mechanics, the hard bans, and the specific things that have already been argued out and settled.

**Read this before writing a single customer-facing line.**

---

## The one idea

> "Think less Kinfolk magazine. Think Kinfolk magazine written by someone who's just finished stretching pizza dough for six hours." — Mel, 28 Jul 2026

The design is editorial and finished. **The words are not.** Copy has to sound like Jordy sat down for five minutes after a shift, not like a beautifully edited brand piece.

> "My remaining edits aren't about making it more casual — they're about making it feel effortless. The best Jordy's copy reads like nobody spent very long writing it, even though it's been carefully refined."

**The failure mode has a name: narrator voice.** Polished, balanced sentences nobody would say out loud. It is the single most common way a draft goes wrong, and it never looks wrong — it looks *good*. That's the trap.

---

## Hard bans — already litigated, don't reopen

| Ban | Why |
|---|---|
| **No prices in campaign email copy.** Not in a dish label, not as a joke. | A price turns an editorial piece into a menu. *(Happy Nights' "$10 beer, wine + margs" in the standing footer is furniture, not story — that one stays.)* |
| **No reply instructions.** | "hit reply and tell us" was cut. Leave the question hanging: *"so, what town next?"* |
| **No other brands.** | *"if maccas grew up"* → *"if a cheeseburger grew up."* |
| **No absolute claims that don't ring true.** | *"nobody's ever asked"* reads false. *"somehow we've never talked about it"* is true and modest. |
| **Never undercut the hero of the piece.** | See below. Got this wrong twice. |
| **No manufactured urgency, no gated offers, no two-tier mechanics.** | See CURRENT_PROMOTIONS.md. |
| **No invented local facts, history or anecdotes.** | A wrong detail about someone's home town costs more than the email earns. A thinner email beats a false one. |

**Untouchable:** CTA *"save me a seat"*. Sign-off *"eat your crust, Jordy"*.

## Self-deprecation has a target, and it isn't the product

Rejected on The Local No. 02 — the **Billinudgel**, Jordy's most popular pizza, the one people travel for:

- ~~"Hot. Sweet. A bit much."~~
- ~~"it shouldn't work."~~
- ~~"three things arguing. somehow it's fine."~~

The flagship had been written as an underdog. Cheek is right, but pointed at the *recipe's excess* or at *Jordy* — never at whether the food is good, and never at all when the item is the hero. "Somehow it's fine" is faint praise for a bestseller.

**The fix isn't bragging** (that trips the clever-but-smug rule). It's to **let the customers make the claim and have Jordy report it, slightly bemused**:

> we kept adding things and nobody stopped us.
>
> turns out that's the one you wanted.
>
> it goes out more than anything else on the menu. some of you drive a fair way for it.

Headline became **"People drive for this one."** — confident, instantly legible, credit sitting with the reader rather than the kitchen.

**Generalise:** popularity, quality and craft are stated as *observations about what customers do*, never as claims about how good Jordy's is. Keeps the modesty. Still lands the point.

## Mechanics

### Little Australian moments
*anyway… · turns out… · somehow · weird little thing · fun little fact · no big story · reckon · just because · yeah… · if you know, you know*

Fragments are good. One-word sentences are good.

### Let Jordy actually talk
The gold standard — Jordy at the bar:

> "Yeah… started naming pizzas after towns years ago. No idea why. Just stuck."

Write *that*. Don't summarise it.

### Pauses — the big structural one
Vary sentence length hard. Stack short lines:

> casuarina.
> fingal.
> mullumbimby.
> stokers.

Use the shortened forms a local actually says — *fingal*, *stokers* — in body copy, even though the map block carries the full menu names. Short paragraphs keep people scrolling.

### The emphatic slot is for emotion, not jokes
The 22px centred italic is reserved for feeling:

> *if you grew up around here, chances are we've named dinner after somewhere you've driven through.*

**The mechanism matters as much as the words.** A dry aside in body copy reads editorial; the same joke in the emphatic italic reads as a punchline and dies. Jokes go in the body. Always.

*"local discount pending."* was rejected — too Instagram.

### Texture, not specs
What it's *like*, not what's on it.

### Headlines: instantly understandable beats clever
Mel chose *"Your local's probably on the menu."* over *"You've probably eaten your town."* If it needs two beats of thinking, it's wrong.

### Subject lines must not smell like marketing
*"ever noticed the names?"* · *"people drive for this one"* · *"half a lime and some capers"* · *"yes. pineapple."*

## Before / after

| Written *(wrong)* | Effortless *(right)* |
|---|---|
| We named one after home. | Your local's probably on the menu. |
| Every pizza we make is a town near here. | fun little fact about our menu. |
| every pizza on it's named after somewhere down the road | every pizza's named after somewhere local. |
| fifteen of them. been up there for years and nobody's ever asked. | somehow we've never talked about it. |
| started doing it ages ago. no idea why. just stuck. | no big story. one day we named one after casuarina, then we just kept going. |
| starting with the one out the front door. | might as well start where it started. |
| it's a cheeseburger, more or less. we're not going to pretend otherwise. | yeah… it's basically a cheeseburger. if a cheeseburger grew up. |
| still thirty bucks. local discount pending. | *(cut — no prices)* |
| hit reply and tell us which town you want next. we read them. | so, what town next? |
| **Hot. Sweet. A bit much.** | **People drive for this one.** |
| **it shouldn't work.** | turns out that's the one you wanted. |
| **three things arguing. somehow it's fine.** | everything at once. that's the whole idea. |

## Still avoid

Clever-but-smug product-brag: *"the one you actually came for"*, *"thank us later"*. Too pleased with themselves.

---

## Voice check before delivering

1. Would a person who works at Jordy's actually say this out loud?
2. Is any sentence balanced and polished in a way nobody speaks?
3. Is the joke in the body, or did it end up in the emphatic slot?
4. Have I undercut the hero of the piece?
5. Is a claim about quality being made by Jordy instead of reported from customers?
6. Have I stated an operational fact I can't source? *(Check BRAND_FACTS.md.)*
7. Could this copy belong to another restaurant?

If a line feels *beautifully written*, look at it again. The craft should be invisible.
