## The tone reference

> "sometimes all you need is a pizza, ten dollar margs, and to ignore your responsibilities."
> — Mel's canonical tone reference, 21 Jul 2026

Permission-giving and relatable. About the **customer's mood** — switch off, escape adult life — not about how good the food is. On the customer's side. Warm, low-stakes, loose.

Other on-tone lines:
- *don't overthink it*
- *you booked for pizza. we'll take it from there*
- *you said you were full. you weren't*
- *some nights you just need a pizza, a marg, and zero responsibilities*

**Why it works:** the whole thing is an observation about their own menu. No research, no claim, no sell. "somehow we've never talked about it" is the honest version of a brag.

### No. 03 — New Italy *(files ready)*
Subject **"this one named itself"** · A/B *"nowhere to hide on this one"* · Preheader *"three things on it. that's the whole pizza."*
Headline **This one / named itself.**

> third one.

>
> so that's what we called the margherita. we didn't think too hard about it.

**Why it works:** the joke was already inside the format — New Italy is the one where the name is almost too convenient. No research needed. ## The Excuse — No. 01 *(written, not built)*

Subject **"half a lime and some capers"** · A/B *"it's wednesday. that's the whole excuse."* · Preheader *"that's what's in your fridge. we've checked."*
Headline **It's Wednesday. That's the whole excuse.**

> you don't need a reason. but if you'd like one: it's wednesday. it's dark by six. there is half a lime and some capers in the fridge and nobody in this house is turning that into dinner. we're open from five. the dough's been going since monday. $10 margs, beer and wine with any pizza, wednesday and thursday — same as always, same for everyone.

**Why it works:** *"half a lime and some capers"* is a real fridge, not a joke about one. And *"same as always, same for everyone"* does the anti-gating work in five words.
