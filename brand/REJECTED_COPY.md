# REJECTED_COPY.md

Copy and concepts that were rejected, and the reason. **The reason is the valuable part** — the specific line matters less than the pattern it represents.

Every entry is real. Nothing here is invented for illustration.

---

## Rejected copy

### "Hot. Sweet. A bit much." · "it shouldn't work." · "three things arguing. somehow it's fine."
**The Local No. 02, Billinudgel. Rejected 28 Jul 2026.**

All three wrote the **flagship as an underdog**. The Billinudgel is Jordy's most popular pizza — the one people travel for. "Somehow it's fine" is faint praise for a bestseller.

**Pattern:** cheek is right, but pointed at the recipe's excess or at Jordy — never at whether the food is good, and never at all when the item is the hero of the piece. *This was got wrong twice before it was got right.*

**Replaced by:** *"People drive for this one."* / *"turns out that's the one you wanted."* / *"everything at once. that's the whole idea."*

### "there's a town down the highway called new italy. italians settled it in the 1880s. we didn't make that up."
**The Local No. 03. Rejected 28 Jul 2026 — Mel: "i dont like this email copy at all. i need a completely different angle."**

Four failures, all worth generalising:

1. **It was a history lesson.** A date and a settlement fact delivered like a plaque on a wall. Textbook narrator voice. *Interesting ≠ in voice.*
2. **"we didn't make that up" is defensive.** Insisting on your own credibility signals the opposite.
3. **The headline needed two beats.** *"Somebody named a town after home first"* — instantly understandable beats clever.
4. **The whole edition rested on an unverified claim,** so it shipped with a blocker attached. Building the spine of a piece on something you have to caveat is a structural mistake, not a copy one.

**Pattern:** when an edition feels thin, **look for the joke inside the format before reaching outside it for material.** The premise already was that the town names just happened — New Italy is the one where the name is almost too convenient. That needed no research at all. Also: cutting the thing you can't stand behind usually makes the piece stronger.

**Tell to watch for:** an edition that opens with *research* rather than *observation*.

## Rejected concepts

### "Ten Tables" — list-only tables with Happy Nights unlocked
**Rejected 28 Jul 2026. Mel: Happy Nights is for everyone — Jordy won't gate it.**

**Generalise: never propose a list-only discount, members-only pricing, or any two-tier customer mechanic for Jordy's.** Neighbourhood venue, everyone on the same terms. Reasons to open come from story, taste or usefulness — never from withholding.

---

### The "Service Notes" / kitchen-docket / Courier system
**Rejected 21 Jul 2026.** Never reach for handwritten notes, tilted dockets, receipt stamps, butcher paper, or rotate transforms. Jordy's is premium coastal editorial, not a fridge note.

---

### Specials as their own email
**Rejected 10 Aug 2026.** A special is furniture, not a story. It goes in the standing ON RIGHT NOW band, never in its own send and never as a second article inside one. Protects send frequency — and frequency is what broke deliverability. See SPECIALS.md.

---

### The July kingfish send — led on "$22"
**Read as a menu listing.** This is the origin of the no-prices rule.

---

### Repeated links + footer nav *(DISSH layout reference, 11 Aug 2026)*
From Mel's DISSH reference: **take** the type-on-photo treatment, the image captions and the non-stacking two-up. **Refuse** the repeated links and the footer nav. One link in the body.

**Removed 28 Jul 2026** — Mel: "a bit too chaotic". The 22 Jul send split 14 clicks across five links. Only the terracotta "save me a seat" button remains, plus the logo = 2 hrefs total.
